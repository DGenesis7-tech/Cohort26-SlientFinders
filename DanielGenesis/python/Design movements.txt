Design movements are styles or trends in design that reflect the cultural, technological, political, and artistic shifts of their time. These movements influence everything from graphic design and architecture to fashion and product design.

Here's an overview of key design movements—especially in the 19th, 20th, and 21st centuries:

⚒️ 1. Arts and Crafts Movement (c. 1880–1920)

Origins: England, as a reaction to industrialization

Key Figures: William Morris, John Ruskin

Characteristics: Handcrafted work, natural materials, simplicity, medieval/gothic inspiration

Impact: Rejected mass production; emphasized craft and beauty in everyday objects

🌼 2. Art Nouveau (c. 1890–1910)

Origins: Europe (especially France, Belgium)

Key Figures: Alphonse Mucha, Hector Guimard

Characteristics: Organic lines, floral motifs, whiplash curves, elegance

Impact: Unified art and design across architecture, interiors, and graphics

🟥 3. Bauhaus (1919–1933)

Origins: Germany (Weimar, Dessau)

Key Figures: Walter Gropius, Paul Klee, László Moholy-Nagy

Characteristics: Functionalism, geometric shapes, minimalism, integration of form and function

Impact: Bridged art and industry, foundational to modernism

🪑 4. Modernism (c. 1920s–1970s)

Key Figures: Le Corbusier, Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, Dieter Rams

Characteristics: “Less is more,” clean lines, minimal ornamentation, rationalism

Impact: Dominant style in mid-20th century architecture, product design, and graphic design

⬛ 5. Art Deco (1920s–1940s)

Origins: France, then global

Key Figures: Émile-Jacques Ruhlmann, Tamara de Lempicka

Characteristics: Luxury, symmetry, geometric forms, metallics, glamour

Impact: Visual identity of the Jazz Age and early modern consumer culture

🟥 6. Swiss / International Typographic Style (c. 1950s–1970s)

Origins: Switzerland

Key Figures: Josef Müller-Brockmann, Max Miedinger

Characteristics: Grid systems, sans-serif fonts (like Helvetica), clarity, objectivity

Impact: Set the tone for corporate branding, signage, and editorial design

🎨 7. Postmodernism (1970s–1990s)

Key Figures: Michael Graves, Ettore Sottsass, David Carson

Characteristics: Playfulness, irony, eclecticism, rejection of Modernism's rigidity

Impact: Embraced diversity in design; popular in 80s and 90s graphics and architecture

📺 8. Digital / Contemporary Design (1990s–Present)

Influences: Tech, internet, social media, AI, UX/UI

Characteristics: Responsive design, flat design, minimalism, interactivity, accessibility

Trends: Brutalism, Neo-minimalism, 3D/AR, retro-futurism, skeuomorphism comeback

Impact: Highly user-centered, data-driven, platform-specific

🌀 9. Eco / Sustainable Design (2000s–Present)

Focus: Environmental impact, renewable materials, ethical production

Designers: Yves Béhar, companies like IKEA and Patagonia

Impact: Integrates social responsibility with aesthetics and function
